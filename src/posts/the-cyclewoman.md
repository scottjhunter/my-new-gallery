  <h1>The Cyclewoman</h1>
  <figure class="blog-page-image">
    <img src="/images/cyclewoman.jpg" alt="Illustration of a woman cycling across a Portland bridge in heavy rain, with traffic and city lights around her.">
  </figure>
  <p>
    The wind was a torrent of rain down the Burnside road,<br>
    The sky was a slate-grey sheet, and the night left a heavy load,<br>
    The Willamette ran dark and deep beneath the bridge's span,<br>
    And Bess came riding, riding, riding--<br>
    Bess came riding along the bike lane.
  </p>
  <p>
    Her helmet was buckled tight beneath her lifted chin,<br>
    Her rain jacket pressed to her body where the cold crept in,<br>
    Her eyes were fixed on the road ahead, the painted line her guide,<br>
    And the city rose up around her--<br>
    The city on every side.
  </p>
  <p>
    She knew every crack in the asphalt, every door that swung too wide,<br>
    Every merge where the trucks came heavy and the mirrors clipped beside,<br>
    She had learned them all the hard way, in the language of the near,<br>
    In the catch of breath, the swerving--<br>
    In the aftertaste of fear.
  </p>
  <p>
    The light on Burnside held her, and the rain came sheeting down,<br>
    And the cars pressed close behind her with their engines' patient sound,<br>
    She felt them there, she always felt them, restless at her back,<br>
    Like something dark and waiting--<br>
    Like something on her track.
  </p>
  <p>
    And still she came riding, riding, riding--<br>
    Still she came riding along the bike lane.
  </p>
  <p>
    <em>Tlot-tlot, tlot-tlot</em> went the tires on the glistening street,<br>
    The rhythm of her breathing and the rhythm of her feet,<br>
    A dog came hard from a gateway, and the world collapsed to sound--<br>
    She pulled left, she felt the wobble--<br>
    Then found the ground.
  </p>
  <p>
    She lay a breath in the rainwater, the cold against her face,<br>
    And the traffic parted slowly, giving just enough of space,<br>
    She rose, she checked her hands, her knees, she lifted up the bike,<br>
    And she came riding, riding, riding--<br>
    She came riding, as she always did. Alike.
  </p>
  <p>
    For no one gives you a medal for arriving safe to work,<br>
    For threading every danger, for the swerve and for the jerk,<br>
    The city does not see her, and the city does not mourn--<br>
    But she comes riding, riding, riding--<br>
    She comes riding through the rain, and through the storm.
  </p>
  <p><em>Poem by Scott J. Hunter.</em></p>
